# Plan Structure

Read this when writing the plan or running its check-ins: the full template, worked
examples of observable measures and of real support, the check-in note format, what each
of the three outcomes requires, and the process and legal traps.

## In this file

- The template
- Observable measures, worked
- Support that is real
- The check-in note
- The three outcomes
- Process and legal traps

## The template

```markdown
# Performance improvement plan — [name]
Period: [start] to [end]. Review on [date]. Manager: [name].

## Why we are here
[Two or three sentences: the gap, and that it has been raised before, with dates. No new
information.]

## What needs to change
[Per gap: behaviour, impact, evidence. Two or three only.]

## What success looks like
[The expected behaviour, specifically, in each area above.]

## How it will be measured
[Per measure: what is observed, by whom, how often, and the threshold. Within the
person's control.]

## Support provided
[Per item: what, from whom, when. Manager time, pairing, mentoring, training, a reduced
load, clearer context.]

## Check-ins
[Weekly, [day and time], with a written note after each.]

## Outcomes at review
- Met: [what happens]
- Partly met: [one extension with a reason, or closure with support]
- Not met: [the process that follows, per local policy]

Signed: [person] [date]. Right to respond in writing attached.
```

## Observable measures, worked

| Vague | Observable, with a threshold |
| --- | --- |
| "Communicate better" | "Posts the weekly design note by Friday; the two dependent teams confirm they were consulted before a review" |
| "Be more reliable" | "Meets the two committed items for the quarter, with a mid-quarter check-in that flags slippage at least a week ahead" |
| "Show more ownership" | "Owns the migration end to end, including the sequencing conversation with the two dependent teams, and reports status without being asked" |
| "Improve code quality" | "Review feedback drops to the level of the team's median over the period, measured on the same rubric the team uses" |

A measure the person cannot influence — team velocity, a project's success, another
person's behaviour — is not a measure of this person and does not belong in their plan.

## Support that is real

Support is the part plans most often omit, and its absence is what makes a plan unfair.

- **Manager time:** a named weekly slot that does not move, not "my door is open".
- **Pairing:** a specific person, for a specific number of hours, on the specific work.
- **Mentoring:** an engineer outside the reporting line, agreed with them and with their
  manager.
- **Training:** the course, the budget, the time to attend it.
- **A reduced load:** the work taken off their plate, named, and where it went.
- **Clearer context:** the expectations, the priorities and the definition of done written
  down, where their absence contributed to the gap.

If none of these can be offered, the honest conclusion is that the gap is not going to
close with a plan, and step 1 applies again.

## The check-in note

One short note per check-in, written the same day. The record is built from these, and a
record reconstructed at the end is worth much less than one written as it happened.

```text
[date] — [person]
Discussed: [what]
Progress: [what improved, against which measure]
Still open: [what has not, against which measure]
Support: [what was provided, what was promised for next week]
Next check-in: [date]
```

Send it to the person, or keep it where the person can see it. A private note is a
surprise waiting to happen.

## The three outcomes

| Outcome | What it requires |
| --- | --- |
| Met | Close it in writing, say so, and keep the support that worked — the underlying reason has not disappeared |
| Partly met | One extension with a written reason and a new end date, or close with continued support. Two extensions is an exit nobody has said out loud |
| Not met | The decision is made; the delivery is `difficult-conversation`, and the process that follows is the local one |

## Process and legal traps

- **Jurisdiction.** The process, the notice and the documentation requirements differ by
  country and by contract. Follow local advice, not this file.
- **Documentation timing.** Notes written at the end read as a reconstruction. Write them
  as the period runs.
- **Consistency.** The same behaviour in another person, treated differently, is the
  substance of a claim. Apply one standard.
- **Protected characteristics.** A plan that disproportionately lands on one group is a
  structural finding, not a set of individual ones. Look at the pattern before concluding
  on the person.
- **Promises made in the plan.** What you commit to as support is now part of the record;
  a promise not delivered weakens every other part of it.
- **The decision to exit.** If the decision is made, run it as an exit with a fair process
  and notice. Do not route it through a plan to manufacture a reason.
