# Ramp Signals

Read this when a ramp looks stalled, or before an early conversation about whether someone
is progressing. The purpose is to stop a manager converting a vague unease into a
conclusion about the person before checking the three cheaper explanations.

A slow ramp and a mismatch look the same in month one. They separate on two things: the
trend week over week, and the kind of difficulty. Someone who is slow but whose questions
are getting deeper is ramping. Someone whose questions are the same in week six as in week
two is not, and the reason is often still not the person.

## Check your own contribution first

Answer these before forming any view. Each one is a common cause, each is fixable, and
each is yours.

| Question | If the answer is no |
| --- | --- |
| Were expectations written down and shared with them? | They have been guessing at the target. Write them, share them, restart the clock on the judgement. |
| Was the context transferred, or assumed? | They are reconstructing three years of reasoning from the code. Schedule the walkthroughs. |
| Was the first task the right size and genuinely completable? | A first task that was secretly hard has told you nothing except that it was hard. |
| Was the buddy actually available? | The questions went unasked. Change the buddy and say why. |
| Did they get specific feedback in week one and week two? | The first feedback they will hear is a concern, which lands as a verdict. |
| Is the environment working properly for them? | You are measuring the setup, not the engineer. |

Where any answer is no, fix it, allow a fair interval, and look again. Whatever the
eventual outcome, this belongs in the written record: it is accurate, and it is part of
what has to change.

## Reading the signals

| Signal | Most likely reading | Least likely reading | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Very few questions, little visible output | Does not feel safe asking, or does not know who to ask | Does not need help | Ask what they are stuck on, specifically; make your own not-knowing visible; check the buddy is available |
| Constant questions about tooling and setup | The environment is broken | Cannot handle the tooling | Fix the environment and the setup document; this is your defect |
| Work is completed but always shaped by someone else | Missing context to shape it themselves | Lacks initiative | Transfer more context, not more supervision; hand them a problem rather than a task and see what happens |
| Long silences followed by large, off-target changes | Reluctant to check in early | Poor judgement | Introduce a mid-task check-in as a norm for everyone, not as a correction for them |
| The same concept re-explained repeatedly | An unwritten concept nobody has explained properly once | A capability gap | Write it down once, properly. Then observe whether it holds |
| Confident output that is wrong in the same way each time | A genuine concern worth investigating now | A slow start | Specific, evidenced feedback immediately, in the terms `growth-review` uses |
| Slow but improving week over week | A normal ramp in a hard codebase | Anything else | Adjust the dates, tell them you have, and leave them alone |
| Progressing technically, isolated socially | Remote defaults are doing what they always do | Not a team fit | Schedule the incidental contact that is not happening by itself |

## When it is a real mismatch

Some hires are wrong, and pretending otherwise wastes the person's career as well as the
team's time. The signs that survive the checks above:

- No trend. Week six looks like week two on the same material, after the environment was
  fixed and the context was transferred.
- Feedback lands but does not change behaviour, repeatedly, on the same specific point.
- The gap is in something the loop claimed to test. That is a finding about the loop as
  well; feed it back into `hiring-loop` rather than only into this person's record.
- Consistent overconfidence in areas where accuracy matters and being wrong is expensive.

Act early rather than waiting for a probation date to force the conversation. A direct
conversation in week six is still about ramping and still has somewhere to go; the same
conversation in week eleven is a process. Move to `growth-review` for the structure of
that conversation — behaviour, impact, evidence, no surprises — and involve whoever owns
employment process where you are before anything becomes formal.

## What to write down as you go

Keep a short running note per joiner, appended weekly, one line per observation with a
date. Reconstructing month one at the end of month three produces the recency problem
`growth-review` exists to prevent, in a situation where the stakes for the person are high
and their tenure is short. The note also records what you fixed, which is what tells the
next joiner's plan what to do differently.
