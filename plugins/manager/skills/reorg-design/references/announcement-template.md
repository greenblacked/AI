# Announcement template

Everything below is written for the managers to adapt, not to read aloud.

## Manager briefing (step 1 of the sequence)

Send the design and talking points the evening before, or hold the briefing in person. Cover:

- The problem and its number, in one paragraph.
- The new structure, who reports to whom, and the effective date.
- The sequence and the timings, down to the hour.
- The list of people each manager will tell, and what is different for each.
- What is not decided yet, and who decides it.
- The questions people are likely to ask, with agreed answers. Where the answer is "not decided", the briefing says so, and everyone says it the same way.
- What to do if someone becomes upset, asks to leave, or asks to be moved.

## The 1:1 (step 2)

Ten to thirty minutes, in person or on video, never by message. Structure:

1. Say the news in the first two sentences: "As of the first of next month the team is being split. You will move to the data platform team, reporting to Sam."
2. Say why, using the number from the problem statement.
3. Say what stays the same.
4. Stop. Let the person react before explaining more.
5. Answer what you can. For what you cannot, say who will answer and by when.
6. Ask what they need from you this week.

Do not apologise for the decision or defend it at length. Do not promise anything that depends on someone else.

## Written announcement (step 7)

```markdown
Subject: Changes to how [group] is organised, effective [date]

What is changing
- [Team X splits into A and B. A owns ..., B owns ...]
- [Managers: ...]

What is not changing
- [Compensation, levels, locations, the roadmap commitments ...]

Why
- [The problem, in one sentence with the number. For example: 11 of our last 14 projects
  needed handoffs between two or more teams, and requests waited a median of six days in
  another team's queue.]

What is still open, and who decides
- [On-call arrangement for the shared service: decided by ..., by ...]
- [Seating or tooling changes: ...]

What happens next
- [Date of the change; team meetings this week; where to ask questions]
- We will recount the same handoff measure at 90 days, on [date], and tell you what we find.
```

## 90-day review agenda

1. The original problem measure, recounted the same way, beside the earlier figure.
2. Regretted attrition in affected teams against the prior baseline.
3. Delivery rate against the baseline, with the expected early dip called out.
4. One-paragraph pulse from each team lead: what is better, what is worse.
5. The reversal criterion read aloud, then the decision: keep, adjust, or reverse.
6. What goes back to the affected people, and who writes it.
