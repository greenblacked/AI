# Review templates and variants

Fill these; do not compose a structure each week. Every heading exists because leaving it
out is a known failure — the capacity line stops the wish list, the not-doing line stops
the undeclared deferral, the prune line is the only thing that ever gives hours back.

## The five-minute version

Use it when the alternative is skipping. It keeps the habit and produces the one output.

    Last week: [the one thing that did not happen, and why]
    Free hours next week: [number]
    This week I will: [1] [2] [3]
    If only one survives: [priority] — blocked [day, time]
    Not doing: [thing]

Nothing else. No collection pass, no inbox, no filing.

## The full weekly template

    # Week of [date]

    ## Collected
    Notes: [items]  Messages: [items]  Calendar: [items]
    Task list rollovers: [items]  Open loops: [the form, the call, the conversation]

    ## Last week, honestly
    | Committed | Outcome | Cause |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | [item] | done / partial / not started | estimate / interruption / dependency /
    |        |                              | avoidance / deprioritised |
    Repeating pattern: [what showed up for the third week running]

    ## Capacity
    Meetings and commitments: [hours]
    Realistic focus time: [hours]
    Disruptions known in advance: [on-call, travel, deadline, family, appointments]

    ## Commitments (3-5, each verifiable on Friday)
    1. [outcome] — blocked [day, time]
    2. [outcome]
    3. [outcome]
    Priority if the week goes badly: [one of the above]

    ## Consciously not doing
    - [thing] — [reason] — [reconsider on date]

    ## Waiting on
    - [person / thing] — [chase on day]

    ## Prune
    - [recurring meeting or commitment cancelled, shortened or made async]

    ## Time-critical before the next review
    - [deadline, booking, renewal, birthday]

## Capacity estimation

Count down from the working week rather than up from zero; counting up flatters.

| Take out | Typical |
| --- | --- |
| Meetings and calls already in the calendar | count them |
| Admin, email, messages, review of other people's work | 4-6 hours |
| Context switching around fragmented days | 30-60 min per additional context per day |
| On-call, if in rotation | half the week's focus time, more after a bad night |
| Travel days | assume no focus work, not a full day of it |
| Recovery after a launch, incident or long trip | half a day, honestly |

Whatever remains is the number to plan against. If it is under five hours, the week takes
one commitment, not four, and saying that out loud is more useful than pretending.

## The monthly variant

Fifteen minutes, appended to that week's review rather than replacing it.

    # [Month] review

    ## What actually changed
    - [outcome a sceptic would accept as evidence, not a list of activity]

    ## Carried all month and still not done
    - [item] → drop / shrink to a first step / schedule with a date

    ## Where the weeks went
    Roughly: [n]% [category], [n]% [category], [n]% [category]
    Is that the split you would choose? [yes / no, and what moves]

    ## Stopping this month
    - [project, commitment or obligation being ended, and how it is ended]

    ## One change to the review itself
    - [add, cut or shorten]

## The quarterly variant

Same shape, longer horizon, plus two questions the monthly cannot answer:

    ## Direction
    Three months of weeks moved: [the thing that matters] / nothing much.
    If a year of quarters like this one passed, would that be acceptable? [answer]

    ## The one big thing for next quarter
    [a single objective, with what would be visible in twelve weeks if it happened]

Keep the answer to the second question to one item. A quarter with three big things
produces three half-finished ones.

## Restarting after missed weeks

Reconstruction is what causes the next miss. The restart is deliberately shallow:

1. Do not read back through the missed period. Ignore it.
2. Sweep once, for five minutes, for anything time-critical that happened while the
   review was not running: deadlines, renewals, unanswered messages from people who
   matter, bills, appointments. Handle or schedule those.
3. Ask one question about the gap: was the slot wrong, was the review too long, or was
   something else going on in life? Fix that one thing before adding anything back.
4. Run the five-minute version. Not the full one — the first review back should be
   easier than the ones that were skipped, or it will be skipped again.
5. Return to the full version the following week if the short one felt thin.
