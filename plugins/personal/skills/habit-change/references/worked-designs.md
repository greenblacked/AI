# Worked designs

Read this once the behaviour is named and the design needs a cue, a floor and an
environment change filled in. Each entry gives a sentence that has survived contact with
ordinary life, the floor version, the environment work in both directions, and the trap
specific to that behaviour. Adapt rather than copy: the anchor has to be something that
already happens in this person's day.

## Exercise

**Sentence.** After I drop the children at school, I will change into kit and go out of the
front door.

**Floor.** Kit on, out of the door, ten minutes in any direction. The session is optional
once outside; the door is the behaviour being trained.

**Toward it.** Kit laid out the night before, in the room where the anchor happens. The
route decided in advance so nothing is chosen while standing in the hall. One pair of shoes
by the door rather than in a cupboard.

**Away from the alternative.** Do not sit down between the anchor and the door — the sofa
is the competing behaviour, and it wins whenever it is on the path.

**The trap.** Distance and pace creep upward on good days until the floor has quietly
become 5 km, and then a busy fortnight breaks it. Restate the floor out loud at each
review. Soreness after a heroic first week is the single most common reason the second week
does not happen.

## Reading

**Sentence.** After I get into bed, I will read one page before touching the phone.

**Floor.** One page. Not one chapter, not ten minutes.

**Toward it.** The book physically on the pillow, so the bed cannot be got into without
moving it. One book at a time, chosen for pull rather than worthiness — an unfinished
improving book is a nightly reminder of failure.

**Away from the alternative.** Phone charging in another room, or at least across the room
and face down. The phone in the bed is not a competing behaviour, it is the same slot
already occupied.

**The trap.** Page counts and reading goals turn the habit into a performance and make an
absorbing slow book feel like falling behind.

## Sleep

**Sentence.** After the 21:45 alarm sounds, I will put the phone on its charger in the
hall.

**Floor.** Phone on the charger. Bedtime itself is not directly controllable and should not
be the tracked behaviour.

**Toward it.** A fixed wake time, including weekends, because the wake time anchors the
whole cycle and is the half a person can actually control. Lights lowered at the same
trigger.

**Away from the alternative.** Charger out of the bedroom. Television off at the same
alarm, because "one more episode" is a decision made by someone already tired.

**The trap.** Tracking sleep with a wearable converts a bad night into an anxious morning
and, for some people, measurably worsens the thing it measures. Track the behaviour — phone
on the charger — not the outcome.

## Phone and scrolling

**Sentence.** When I sit down on the sofa, the phone goes in the drawer.

**Floor.** The phone in the drawer for the first ten minutes.

**Toward it.** A replacement for the hands and the two minutes: the book, the instrument,
the crossword, physically in the same room as the sofa.

**Away from the alternative.** Notifications off at the operating-system level rather than
per app. The worst offenders deleted and reachable only through a browser, logged out, so
every use costs a password. Greyscale helps some people and does nothing for others; try it
for a week and keep it only if it earned its place.

**The trap.** Screen-time limits that can be dismissed with a tap add a ritual rather than
a step. If the limit is dismissible in under three seconds, it is decoration.

## Practising a skill

**Sentence.** After I pour the first coffee, I will play one scale.

**Floor.** One scale, one sentence, one sketch, one problem.

**Toward it.** The instrument out of its case and on a stand; the file already open from
last night; the next exercise chosen at the end of the previous session so the session
starts with a decision already made.

**Away from the alternative.** The laptop's other windows closed the night before, because
the practice slot loses to email at exactly the moment it starts.

**The trap.** Practising only when a long stretch is available. The long stretch is rare,
and waiting for it is how six months pass. Short daily beats long weekly for skill
retention and, more to the point, it is what actually happens.

## Medication adherence

**Sentence.** After I switch on the kettle in the morning, I take the tablet.

**Floor.** There is no floor; the dose is the dose. This is the one case where shrinking
does not apply, so the entire design load falls on the cue and the environment.

**Toward it.** A weekly pill organiser in the place the anchor happens — it doubles as the
tracker, since a filled compartment is visible evidence of a miss without any recording
step. A second organiser in the travel bag.

**Away from the alternative.** Nothing to remove; instead add a backstop alarm for the same
time, since the cost of a missed dose is asymmetric.

**The trap.** Treating a missed dose as a habit problem when it is a supply problem. Track
where the misses fall: away from home, or after the pack ran out, are different faults with
different fixes. For any medication where timing or missed doses carry clinical
consequences, the prescriber decides the recovery rule, not this skill.

## Drinking less

**Sentence.** On a weeknight, after I put the children to bed, I pour a soda and lime and
sit down.

**Floor.** The replacement drink is poured. The point of the design is to occupy the slot,
not to resist it.

**Toward it.** The alternative bought, cold and visible, before the week starts. A specific
glass, because the ritual is doing a lot of the work.

**Away from the alternative.** Nothing in the house on weeknights, bought in single units
rather than in cases, and the route home changed if it passes the shop. Removing supply
beats resisting supply.

**The trap.** Counting units without changing the situation. Also: this design is for
moderate drinking with a habitual trigger. Dependence is a clinical matter — unplanned
withdrawal from heavy alcohol use can be dangerous — so where there is physical dependence,
morning drinking, or a failed attempt with withdrawal symptoms, refer to a clinician and
stop designing.
