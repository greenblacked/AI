# Documents and money

Read this in the final week before departure, and again the night before packing. It is a
checklist rather than a discussion, because it is used when there is no time left to think.

## The document set

For each traveller:

- **Passport**, checked against the destination's validity and blank-page rules.
- **Visa or electronic authorisation**, with its reference number recorded separately from
  the email it arrived in.
- **Boarding passes and booking references** for every leg, saved offline. An app that
  needs a signal is not a boarding pass at a rural station.
- **Accommodation confirmations** with the address written in the local language and
  script. A taxi driver cannot read a screenshot in your alphabet.
- **Insurance policy number and the insurer's 24-hour emergency line**, which is a
  different number from the customer service line and is the only one staffed at 3am.
- **Onward or return ticket**, if the destination requires proof of it.
- **Driving licence and any international permit**, if driving.
- **Vaccination certificates**, where mandatory.
- **Consent letter and birth certificate** for a child travelling without both parents.

## The copy strategy

Three copies, in three places, because the failure being planned for is losing the bag with
the documents in it:

1. **The originals**, carried on the person or in the cabin bag, never in checked luggage.
2. **Paper photocopies** of the passport photo page, the visa, the insurance policy and the
   first accommodation booking, kept in a different bag from the originals. Paper works
   without battery, signal or a working phone.
3. **Encrypted digital copies**, in a password manager or an encrypted note, accessible
   from a browser on a borrowed device. Unencrypted passport scans in a photo roll or an
   email inbox are an identity-theft kit waiting for the phone to be stolen.

Leave a fourth copy, plus the itinerary, with someone at home.

## Cards and cash

- **Two cards, two networks, carried separately.** One card is a single point of failure:
  cards get blocked by fraud systems, swallowed by machines, cloned and stolen. Keep the
  backup somewhere the primary is not — a different bag, the hotel safe.
- **Check network acceptance before relying on a card.** Visa and Mastercard are broadly
  accepted; American Express and Discover are thinly accepted outside their home markets
  and are a poor choice as the only card.
- **Prefer a card with no foreign transaction fee.** The typical charge is around 2.5–3%
  on every transaction, which is a meaningful fraction of a trip's spending.
- **Know your card's cash withdrawal terms.** Many cards charge a cash advance fee and
  begin charging interest immediately on withdrawals, even when the balance is cleared.
- **Carry enough local cash for the first day**, sized to a taxi, a meal and a contingency.
  Airport exchange desks have the worst rates available anywhere; use a bank machine in the
  terminal instead, declining the machine's own conversion offer.
- **Tell the bank where you are going** only if the bank still requires it; many no longer
  do, but a blocked card on day one is expensive either way. Note the bank's overseas
  contact number, which will not be a freephone number that works from abroad.
- **Split the cash** between the person and the bag, in two places.

## Dynamic currency conversion

When a card terminal or a bank machine abroad offers to charge you in your home currency,
decline it every time. That offer is dynamic currency conversion, and the rate applied is
set by the merchant's processor with a margin typically in the range of 3% to 7% over the
rate the card network would have used. Paying in the local currency lets your own card
network convert at its own rate, which is close to interbank.

The offer is often phrased so that accepting looks like the safe, certain choice, and on a
bank machine it can appear as a large "accept conversion" button against a small decline
link. Decline is always correct.

## Payment quirks worth checking per destination

Card and cash norms vary more than travellers expect, and getting this wrong wastes hours:

- Some countries are effectively cashless, where carrying much cash is pointless.
- Some are effectively cash-only outside the largest cities, where an assumption that a
  card works strands you at a rural guesthouse.
- Some run domestic card networks that foreign cards do not join, so a card works in a
  hotel and fails in a supermarket.
- Contactless limits, tipping norms, and whether restaurants can split a bill across cards
  all differ, and the last one matters for a group trip.
- Some countries have strict limits on the amount of currency that may be imported or
  exported, with a declaration requirement above a threshold.

Check the current position for the specific destination; this is a fast-moving area.

## Health and medication

- Carry prescription medication in **original labelled packaging**, in the cabin bag, in
  quantities for the trip plus several spare days.
- Carry a **copy of the prescription** or a letter from the prescriber naming the generic
  drug, which travels better than a brand name.
- Check whether anything you carry is **controlled at the destination**. Some common
  prescriptions — including certain stimulants, strong painkillers and some sleep and
  anxiety medications — are restricted or banned in specific countries and may require an
  import permit applied for weeks in advance.
- Split medication across two bags where quantity allows, so a lost bag is not a lost
  course.
- Note the generic names of anything taken regularly, plus allergies and blood group, in a
  document a stranger could find.

## Driving

- Check whether an international driving permit is required. Where it is, it must usually
  be obtained in the country that issued the licence, before departure, and it is not
  valid without the licence alongside it.
- Check what the hire company requires beyond the licence: a credit card in the driver's
  name, a minimum age, a minimum licence-held period, and sometimes an additional-driver
  registration that is not optional if the second person ever drives.
- Understand the excess and whether the waiver sold at the desk duplicates cover you
  already hold. Standalone excess insurance bought in advance is usually much cheaper than
  the desk's version.
- Check the mandatory equipment rules for the country, which in parts of Europe include
  warning triangles, high-visibility vests, breathalysers or winter tyres by date.

## Connectivity

- Decide the approach before departure: a roaming add-on, a local physical card, or an
  embedded profile bought online. The last works only on a device that supports it and is
  not locked to a carrier.
- Keep the home number reachable if it receives bank verification messages, because losing
  access to those while abroad locks you out of the accounts you need most.
- Download offline maps, the accommodation addresses, boarding passes and a translation
  language pack before leaving, on the assumption that the first hours have no data.

## The emergency set

Written down, on paper, in the bag that is not the one with the phone in it:

- The insurer's 24-hour emergency assistance number and the policy number.
- The bank's overseas number for each card, plus the card-blocking number.
- The nearest embassy or consulate for each traveller's nationality, with its address.
- Local emergency numbers, which are not 911 or 112 everywhere.
- One contact at home, with the country code written out.
- The first night's accommodation address in the local script.

## The night before

- [ ] Every passport checked against the destination rule, not just the expiry date.
- [ ] Visas and authorisations approved, references recorded outside the email.
- [ ] Boarding passes and confirmations downloaded for offline use.
- [ ] Paper and encrypted copies made; a set left with someone at home.
- [ ] Two cards on different networks, carried separately; first-day cash obtained.
- [ ] Medication packed in the cabin bag in labelled packaging, prescription copy included.
- [ ] Insurance in force, with the emergency number on paper.
- [ ] Check-in complete, seats confirmed, bag allowance checked against the lowest-allowance
      operating carrier on the ticket.
- [ ] Departure terminal and airport confirmed on the day, not at booking.
- [ ] Arrival day deliberately empty.
