# Publication Checklist

Run each pass separately. Trying to check argument, correctness and style at once hides
errors behind fluent prose.

## Reader and argument

- [ ] One specific reader is named.
- [ ] The reader promise describes an action or decision.
- [ ] The opening states the result, tension or mechanism.
- [ ] Every section advances the primary claim.
- [ ] Chronology appears only where sequence is causal.
- [ ] The conclusion adds application or limits rather than repeating the introduction.

## Evidence and accuracy

- [ ] Every number has units, population, window, comparison and source.
- [ ] First-person claims trace to supplied experience or artefacts.
- [ ] External claims use primary sources where available.
- [ ] Inferences are labeled and separated from source statements.
- [ ] Opinions are not presented as measured facts.
- [ ] Versions and dates appear where behavior may change.
- [ ] Every `[TK]` is resolved or removed; any remaining marker makes the article not ready.
- [ ] Links point to the source page, not a search result.

## Reproduction

- [ ] Prerequisites and permissions are stated.
- [ ] Commands have the flags the prose claims.
- [ ] Expected output or verification follows each meaningful operation.
- [ ] Destructive commands have scope, preconditions and recovery guidance.
- [ ] Code excerpts are complete enough to run or clearly labeled pseudocode.
- [ ] Production hardening is not implied by a simplified tutorial.

## Security, privacy and employment

- [ ] No secret, credential, token, internal URL, private hostname or account identifier appears.
- [ ] Customer, employee and incident details are approved or anonymised.
- [ ] Security-sensitive topology and defensive gaps are safe to publish.
- [ ] Employer ownership, review and disclosure policies were considered.
- [ ] Sponsorship, affiliate relationships and material conflicts are disclosed.
- [ ] AI assistance is disclosed when the author or publication requires it.

## Structure and accessibility

- [ ] Heading levels are ordered and descriptive.
- [ ] Paragraphs carry one idea and are short enough to scan.
- [ ] Tables have headers and make sense when read linearly.
- [ ] Diagrams have alt text or an adjacent textual explanation.
- [ ] Images add information and include attribution or license where required.
- [ ] Code blocks name the language and do not rely on color alone.
- [ ] Link text describes the destination.

## Voice and compression

- [ ] The author could defend every sentence aloud.
- [ ] Generic openings and throat-clearing are removed.
- [ ] Adjectives such as significant, robust and scalable are replaced with mechanisms or evidence.
- [ ] Repeated conclusions appear once.
- [ ] Vendor vocabulary is used only where technically necessary.
- [ ] The article states what did not work without manufacturing drama.

## Metadata and handoff

- [ ] Title accurately reflects the proven argument.
- [ ] Description makes the reader promise in one or two sentences.
- [ ] Slug is short, stable and free of dates unless the date matters.
- [ ] Publication date and last-updated policy are known.
- [ ] Canonical URL, tags and social metadata are included when the platform supports them.
- [ ] Source ledger and private disclosure notes are delivered separately from public prose.
- [ ] A final preview checks mobile layout, code overflow, tables, images and links.

## Final verdict

Choose one:

- **Ready:** no unresolved accuracy, privacy or reproduction issue.
- **Ready after named fixes:** list the exact fixes and owner.
- **Not ready:** the primary claim lacks evidence or publication permission.

Do not publish a draft with unresolved privacy or security questions merely because its
prose is finished.
