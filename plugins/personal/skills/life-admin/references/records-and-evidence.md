# Records and evidence

Read this when preparing a submission, and again the moment a process turns into a dispute.
The purpose of a record is not tidiness: it is that the organisation's default position,
when something goes wrong, is that it never happened, and a record is what makes that
position unavailable to them.

## One file per process

One folder, named for the process and the organisation, containing five things and nothing
else:

1. **The requirement**, as captured from the authoritative source: the page or form notes,
   saved as a PDF with the date you read it, plus the form's own issue or version number.
2. **The submission**, exactly as sent: every page, the covering letter, the envelope and
   address, and the postage or portal receipt.
3. **The contact log**, one file, appended to and never rewritten.
4. **Correspondence**, in date order, including the envelopes where a postmark matters.
5. **A one-page chronology**, which is what you hand to an ombudsman, a regulator or a
   professional, and which is the single highest-value artefact in the folder.

Keep the chronology current as you go. Reconstructing it eight months later from an email
inbox takes an afternoon and loses the details that decide the case.

## The contact log

Every interaction, written down at the time, not later. The fields are not optional,
because each one is load-bearing when the account is disputed:

| Field | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Date and time | Lets them find the call in their own systems, which are indexed by time. |
| Channel and number or address used | Distinguishes the general line from the complaints team, which is a different process. |
| The person's full name and team | "A man I spoke to" is not evidence. A name and a team is. |
| The call or contact reference | Most organisations generate one and few volunteer it. Ask for it. It is how the next person finds the notes. |
| What you asked | Fixes the question, so a reply about something else is visibly a reply about something else. |
| What they said, in their words | Paraphrase loses the commitment. Quote the promise. |
| What happens next, and by when | The commitment with a date on it is the thing you chase against. |
| Whether you confirmed it in writing | An unconfirmed call is a memory; a confirmed one is a record. |

Note also when a call is recorded — most are, and the recording is usually something you
can later ask them to retrieve.

## Confirming a phone call in writing

This is the highest-return habit in the whole procedure and takes three minutes.

Send a short message the same day, to an address that belongs to the organisation rather
than to the individual, saying when you spoke, to whom, what was agreed and what happens
next. The value is not that they agree — it is that they had the opportunity to correct it
and did not. An uncorrected written account of a conversation carries far more weight than
either party's later recollection.

Keep it to four sentences. A long email invites a long argument; a short factual one
invites either silence, which helps you, or a correction, which is information.

## Proving a submission

| Method | What it proves | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signed-for or recorded delivery | That it arrived, when, and who took it | Anything with a deadline or a fee attached. Keep the receipt with the tracking number, not only the number. |
| Online portal with a submission reference | Receipt, timestamp and content | Always preferable where offered; screenshot the confirmation screen as well as noting the reference. |
| Email to a monitored address | Receipt, timestamp and content, subject to their denial of receipt | Good, and better with a read receipt or a follow-up chase that quotes it. |
| Hand delivery with a stamped copy | Receipt and date | Counters that will stamp your copy; ask for it explicitly. |
| Ordinary post | Nothing at all | Never, for anything that matters. |

Photograph or scan the complete pack before it goes, including the covering letter, and
including the addressed envelope. Where originals must be sent, record every document
number first, because replacing a lost original starts with knowing what it was.

Note the date they **received** it, not the date you sent it. Every published response time
runs from receipt, and some run only from the point they judge the application complete —
check which, because it changes every subsequent date.

## Writing a letter that gets a substantive reply

Five properties, in this order:

1. **The reference number in the subject line**, every time, so it is routed rather than
   triaged.
2. **A one-paragraph chronology of facts with dates.** No adjectives. Dates and events.
3. **The specific thing that is wrong**, stated once, in terms of what was promised or
   required versus what happened.
4. **The outcome you want**, named concretely: a decision, a refund of a stated amount, a
   correction to a record, an apology in writing. A letter without a requested outcome
   gets a reply that resolves nothing.
5. **A date by which you expect a response**, and what you will do if it passes. Naming the
   next stage — the complaints process, the ombudsman, the regulator — changes who reads
   the letter.

Leave out the frustration. It is legitimate and it is also the thing that lets a reply
answer your tone instead of your point.

## Template: first written chase

```text
Subject: [Reference number] — response overdue, submitted [date]

I submitted [what] on [date]; you confirmed receipt on [date] under reference [number].
Your published processing time is [range], which expired on [date].

Please confirm by [date, 10 working days out] either the decision, or the date on which
the decision will be made and what is outstanding.

If I do not have that by [date] I will raise this through your formal complaints process.
```

## Template: formal complaint

```text
Subject: Formal complaint — [reference number]

I am making a formal complaint under your complaints procedure.

What happened, in date order:
- [date] — [event]
- [date] — [event]

What went wrong: [the specific failure, once, against what was promised or required]

What I want: [the concrete outcome, with an amount or an action]

Please acknowledge this complaint and confirm the reference. I understand your final
response is due within [the published window]. If it is not resolved by then I will refer
the matter to [the named ombudsman or regulator].
```

Use the word "complaint" explicitly. Many organisations only start the timed, logged
process when the word appears, and a message that reads as a chase is handled as a chase.

## Template: confirming a call

```text
Subject: [Reference number] — confirming our call today

To confirm our call at [time] today with [full name] in [team], call reference [number]:

- You said [what they said].
- You agreed [what was agreed].
- You said [what happens next] would happen by [date].

Please correct anything here that does not match your record.
```

## Getting their version of events

When an account is disputed, you are usually entitled to ask for what the organisation
holds about you. In many jurisdictions a data subject access request obliges them to
provide your personal data, typically free and within a statutory period of about a month,
and in practice that often includes case notes, internal messages about your case and call
recordings.

Two things make one useful rather than a pile of paper: ask for a defined period and
subject matter rather than everything, and ask specifically for call recordings and case
notes by name. Check the current rules and deadline in the relevant jurisdiction before
quoting them.

## How long to keep what

| Record | Keep for |
| --- | --- |
| The submission pack and proof of posting | Until the process is closed and the outcome is in hand, then a further period matching the appeal or limitation window. |
| Correspondence and the contact log | The life of the matter plus the limitation period for any claim arising from it. |
| Tax, property and employment records | The statutory retention period in the jurisdiction, which is usually years and longer than people assume. |
| Insurance policies and claims | The policy term plus the claim limitation period, because claims can be made after the policy ends. |
| Anything relating to a matter that could reach a court | Until it is definitively closed, and longer if in doubt. Deleting during a live dispute is the one irrecoverable mistake. |

Store the folder where it survives a lost laptop and a lost phone, and keep paper for the
documents whose originals matter.
