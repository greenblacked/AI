# Recurring admin

Read this when setting up the recurring side for the first time, or after a renewal has
been missed. The premise is narrow: most administrative emergencies are not complicated
processes, they are simple processes started too late, and the fix is an inventory plus two
alerts rather than a system.

## Why renewals become emergencies

Three reasons, and all three are structural rather than personal:

1. **The reminder comes from the organisation that benefits from your inaction.** An
   insurer's renewal notice arrives shortly before the renewal, at a price that assumes you
   will not shop around, and the window to act is deliberately narrow.
2. **The lead time is invisible.** The renewal itself takes twenty minutes; the appointment,
   the medical, the counter-signature or the records request takes weeks, and the deadline
   is set against the twenty minutes.
3. **Nothing connects the expiry dates.** They live on the documents, which are filed, and a
   date that exists only on a filed document is a date nobody sees.

With personal administration already running at roughly nine hours a week and a further
three hours a week spent thinking about it, the recurring category is where the thinking
hours concentrate: a known obligation with an unknown date produces low-grade background
load continuously, and an inventory converts it into one line in a calendar.

## The inventory

One list, one place, reviewed once a year. Columns:

| Field | Note |
| --- | --- |
| Item | What it is, and the reference or policy number. |
| Organisation | Who decides, and the contact route that works. |
| Expiry or renewal date | The actual date on the document, not the month you remember. |
| Lead time | The realistic total from starting to holding the new one. |
| Start date | Expiry minus lead time. This is the date that goes in the calendar. |
| Auto-renews? | And if so, when the cancellation window closes. |
| Cost | Current, and whether it is a reverting introductory rate. |
| Where the document lives | Physical location or file path. |

The start date is the only column that matters day to day. Everything else exists so that
the start date can be calculated and trusted.

## What belongs on it

Work through these categories rather than trying to remember:

- **Identity and travel**: passports for every household member, national identity
  documents, visas and residence permits, electronic travel authorisations tied to a
  passport.
- **Driving and vehicles**: licence, vehicle registration, roadworthiness test, road tax,
  motor insurance, breakdown cover.
- **Insurance**: home, contents, travel, life, health, pet, professional indemnity, and any
  policy with an annual review date.
- **Property and tenancy**: tenancy end and notice period, mortgage fixed-rate expiry,
  service contracts, safety certificates a landlord owes or a homeowner needs.
- **Professional**: registrations, memberships, certifications with continuing requirements,
  mandatory training, background checks with an expiry.
- **Financial**: fixed-rate savings maturity, introductory rates that revert, cards with
  expiry dates that break standing payments, tax filing and payment dates.
- **Household and subscriptions**: energy contract end dates, broadband and mobile contract
  ends, annual subscriptions, warranties and guarantees with a registration or claim window.
- **Health**: prescriptions needing review, screening appointments, vaccinations with a
  validity period.

## Lead times to attach

Confirm each for your own case; these are planning defaults, not facts:

| Item | Plan for |
| --- | --- |
| Passport renewal | Weeks, and longer in peak season. The passport is unavailable while processing, which blocks anything needing it. |
| Driving licence renewal | Weeks, and longer where a medical report is required. |
| Anything needing a records office certificate | Weeks, with expedited options costing more and still taking days. |
| Anything needing an appointment | The first available slot, which is the real constraint. |
| Insurance shopping | Two to three weeks before renewal — quotes are often cheapest a couple of weeks out rather than on the day. |
| Mortgage or fixed-rate switch | Months; offers are commonly reservable well before the current rate ends. |
| Professional registration | The scheme's own window, plus time to complete any outstanding requirement. |

## The two-alert pattern

For every item, two calendar entries, both with the reference number and the organisation
in the title so the entry itself is actionable:

1. **At the start date** — expiry minus lead time. Title it with the verb: "Start passport
   renewal — expires [date]".
2. **One week before the deadline** — the backstop, which catches the first alert being
   dismissed on a busy day.

For anything that auto-renews, a **third** alert before the cancellation window closes,
which is usually earlier than the renewal date itself.

Put them in the calendar that actually notifies you, rather than a task list you open
occasionally. A renewal alert in an unopened app is the same as no alert.

## Auto-renewal traps

- **The loyalty penalty.** Renewal quotes are frequently higher than the new-customer price
  for the identical product from the same provider. Shopping around, or simply asking for
  the new-customer price, is usually worth more per hour than almost anything else on this
  list.
- **Silent renewal at a worse rate.** Introductory rates on energy, broadband, savings and
  mortgages revert to a standard rate that can be substantially higher. The reversion date
  belongs on the inventory as if it were an expiry.
- **Cancellation windows that close before renewal.** Some contracts require notice a set
  period ahead, so the day the renewal notice arrives can already be too late.
- **Auto-renewal that lapses instead.** Where a card expires or a direct debit fails, cover
  can end without anyone noticing. Check that the payment method on file is current
  whenever a card is replaced.
- **Cover that changes on renewal.** An excess, a limit or an exclusion can be altered in
  the renewal documents. The price is not the only thing that moved.

## The annual review

Once a year, in a fixed month, spend an hour on the whole list:

1. Confirm every expiry date against the actual document, not from memory.
2. Recalculate the start dates and re-create the alerts.
3. Cancel what is no longer used. Unused subscriptions and overlapping cover are the
   cheapest savings available and they only surface in a review.
4. Check what changed: a new household member, a moved address, a changed name, a new
   vehicle, a new professional obligation.
5. Check that the documents are where the inventory says they are.

Attach it to something already fixed in the year — a tax deadline, the start of a school
year, a birthday — because a review with no anchor drifts and then stops.

## Batching

Group renewals that need the same evidence and do them together, but only where one does
not require surrendering a document another needs. Proof of address, identity documents and
counter-signatures are the common shared inputs, and obtaining them once for three
processes is most of the saving. Where an original must be surrendered, run those processes
in series and note the dependency on the inventory so a future year does not repeat the
collision.

## Where the documents live

One physical location for originals — certificates, deeds, policies with original wording —
and one digital location for everything else, backed up and reachable from a device that is
not the one in your pocket. Record both in the inventory. A document whose location is
unknown is functionally lost, and replacing it is its own multi-week process at the worst
possible moment.
