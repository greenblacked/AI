# Win-loss interview guide

Read this before contacting buyers: the request, consent and notes, the question set for
each outcome, the follow-ups that get past "price", and the sheet each interview is coded
onto.

## The request

Send the request from the interviewer, not from the account executive, within two weeks of
the close so the interview lands inside 30 days. Keep it short: who you are, that you are
not in sales, that the call is 30 minutes, that you want to learn how they made the
decision and will not try to reopen it, and how the notes will be used. For a lost or
stalled deal, say plainly that nobody will follow up with a sales pitch, and keep that
promise.

Offer a small thank-you only if your company's policy and the buyer's allow it; many
public-sector and regulated buyers cannot accept one.

## Consent and notes

Ask before recording, and record only with a yes. Say who will see the notes. Store quotes
without the buyer's name in anything shared beyond the review team, and keep the
identified notes only as long as the review needs them.

## The opening

"I'm not here to change your mind, and nothing you say will be held against anyone on our
side. I want to understand how the decision was made, from the beginning."

Then stay quiet longer than feels comfortable. The first answer is the summary; the second
is the story.

## The timeline questions

Ask these in order. They rebuild the decision as a sequence of events, which is harder to
tidy than a verdict.

1. What was happening that made you start looking? Why then and not six months earlier?
2. Who was involved, and who had the final say? Who joined late?
3. What options did you consider, including doing nothing or building it yourselves?
4. What did you need to be true to choose one? Which of those mattered most?
5. Was there a moment when the front-runner changed? What caused it?
6. What happened in the last two weeks before the decision?
7. What nearly made you choose differently?
8. If you were advising us, what one thing would you tell us?

## Outcome-specific questions

**Won.** What did we do that the others did not? What almost lost it for us? What did you
have to convince people internally about, and what did you use to do it?

**Lost to a competitor.** What did they show you that convinced you? Where did we fall
short against what you needed? Was there anything we said that turned out not to matter?

**Lost to build.** What made building feel safer or cheaper? What would have had to be
different for buying to win?

**No decision.** What changed between starting and stopping? Who would have had to agree
for it to go ahead? Is the problem still there, and what is it costing you now? This is the
interview most likely to reveal that the buyer never had an urgent enough reason, or that
nobody owned the decision.

## Getting past "it came down to price"

Price is rarely the whole reason; it is the reason that is easiest to give. Follow up:

- "Price compared to what? What would the right price have bought you?"
- "If the prices had been the same, who would you have chosen, and why?"
- "Was it the total, the pricing model, or that the value was hard to justify internally?"

The answers usually sort into three different findings: the value case was not made, the
pricing metric did not fit how they buy, or the price really was out of range for the
segment. Each goes to a different owner.

## The coding sheet

Code each interview onto one row so deals can be counted:

| Field | Values |
| --- | --- |
| Deal id, segment, size | From the CRM |
| Outcome | Won, lost to competitor (name), lost to build, no decision, disqualified |
| CRM reason | As recorded before the interview |
| Trigger | What started the search |
| Deciding criterion | One, in the buyer's terms |
| Criteria where we were stronger | List |
| Criteria where we were weaker | List |
| CRM reason matched buyer's account | Yes, partly, no |
| Quote | One, without the buyer's name |

Have two people code the first ten interviews independently and compare. Disagreement on
the deciding criterion means the criterion definitions are too loose; fix them before
coding the rest.
