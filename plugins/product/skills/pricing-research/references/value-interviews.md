# Value interviews and the economic value worksheet

Contents: who to interview, the interview guide, testing a price in conversation, the economic value worksheet, and reading the results.

## Who to interview

Eight to fifteen people per segment is usually enough to see whether the value story repeats. Recruit current customers, recent evaluators who did not buy, and people using the alternative. Book buyers and users as separate conversations, even at the same company; a user in the room changes what a buyer will say about budget, and the reverse.

Record for each: segment, company size, role (buyer, user, both), current alternative, and whether they have bought something comparable in the last year.

## Interview guide

Keep price out of the first two thirds. The point is to learn the cost of the status quo in the customer's own numbers.

### The current way (both roles)

- Walk me through the last time you had to do this. When was it, who was involved, how long did it take?
- What do you use today? What does it cost, including people's time?
- What goes wrong, and what does it cost when it does? When did that last happen?
- What have you tried instead, and why did you stop?

#### The purchase path (buyers)

- What was the last tool or service you bought for this kind of work? What did it cost and who approved it?
- At what amount does a purchase need someone else's sign-off, or a procurement review?
- Which budget would this come from? Is that budget set for this year already?
- What did you have to show to get the last purchase approved?

##### Value (both roles)

- If this problem disappeared, what would you do with the time or money?
- Which part of the product would you miss first if it were taken away?

## Testing a price in conversation

Only at the end, and only with specific numbers. Pick two or three prices that bracket your working range, and ask about the one in the middle first:

- "At 400 a month for a team, who would need to approve this, and how long would that take?"
- "Would that come from your budget or someone else's?"
- "At 900 a month, what changes?"

Listen for the purchase path changing (a new approver, a procurement review, a budget cycle) rather than for "yes" or "no". The price at which the path changes is often more decisive than any stated ceiling. Do not argue with a reaction or explain why the price is fair; you are measuring, not selling.

## Economic value worksheet

Fill it per segment from the interviews, in the customer's own figures where you have them. Every line carries its source.

| Line | How to estimate | Example (per team per month) | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reference value | What the next-best alternative costs: current tool spend, or hours × loaded hourly cost | 600 | Six interviews: spreadsheet plus 10 hours a month of an analyst |
| Positive differentiation | Net worth of what the product does better: time saved beyond the alternative, errors avoided × their cost, revenue enabled | +900 | Four interviews: two reporting errors a quarter at about 1,350 each |
| Negative differentiation | What it does worse or costs to adopt: switching effort, training, missing features | −200 | Migration estimate from two buyers, spread over a year |
| Economic value | Sum of the three | 1,300 | |
| Capture at 10 to 30 per cent of value created | Applied to the value created beyond the reference: (economic value − reference value) × capture, added to the reference | 670 to 810 | Heuristic |

Two ways of applying the capture heuristic are in use: as a share of the whole economic value, or as the reference value plus a share of only the value created beyond it. They are not close: on the example the first gives 130 to 390, below the 600 the customer already spends on the alternative, and the second gives 670 to 810. The second is the one shown above, because a price below the reference value gives away money the customer was already paying. State which you used.

## Reading the results

- **The value story repeats** across most interviews in a segment, with similar cost figures: the segment is real for pricing purposes and the worksheet is usable.
- **The figures vary by an order of magnitude**: you probably have two segments. Split them and price separately.
- **Users describe high value, buyers describe no budget**: the price is not the problem yet; the product does not have a buyer in this segment.
- **The purchase path changes at a threshold below your working range**: decide deliberately whether to price under it (faster, self-serve sale) or above it (a sales-led motion with the cost that brings).
