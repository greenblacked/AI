## Recruitment message

Send it from a named person, not a no-reply address. Keep the incentive unconditional.

```text
Subject: 45 minutes on how you came to choose [product]?

You started using [product] on [month]. We are trying to understand the story of how
teams like yours decide to change the way they do [job] -- not feedback on the product,
just what happened, in your words.

It takes 45 minutes on a call. With your permission we would record it for our notes
only; we remove names before anything is shared inside the company. As a thank-you we
will send [incentive] whatever you tell us.

Would any of these times work? [three slots]
```

## Opening script

1. Thank them and confirm the time they have.
2. Ask for consent to record, say who will hear the recording, how long it is kept, and that they can stop or skip anything.
3. Frame it: "We are not testing you and there are no right answers. Think of it as a documentary about how you came to buy. The small details help most, even the ones that seem boring."
4. Confirm their role and what they were using before.

## Questions by stage

Start at the purchase, then go back to the first thought and walk forward.

### The purchase (anchor)

- When did you actually sign up or pay? What day was it, roughly what time?
- Where were you? Who else was involved?
- What did you have to do to get it approved?

#### First thought

- When did you first think the way you were doing [job] was not working? What was happening that day?
- What had just gone wrong, or what was about to?
- What did you do about it at the time, if anything?

##### Passive looking

- Between that first thought and starting to search, what did you notice? Anything someone mentioned, an advert, a post?
- How long did that period last?

###### Active looking

- What happened that made you start searching properly? Was there a date or a deadline?
- What did you look at? How did you find each one?
- What did you rule out, and what was the moment you ruled it out?
- How much time did the search take, and who else did you bring in?

###### Deciding

- What made you choose this one on that day rather than a week later?
- Was there anything that nearly stopped you? What did you worry might go wrong?
- Who had to agree? What did you tell them?
- What did you give up or stop using?

###### First use

- Tell me about the first time you used it for real. What were you trying to get done?
- What did you expect to happen? What actually happened?
- What did you do with the old tool or process?

## Probing moves

| They say | You do |
| --- | --- |
| "We always..." | "Take me to the last time that happened. What day was it?" |
| "It was just better" | "Better than what, at what moment? What were you comparing it with on screen?" |
| A feature name | "What were you trying to do when that mattered?" |
| "I don't remember" | Anchor on a nearby fixed point: a holiday, a quarter end, a new hire, a release |
| A long pause | Wait. Do not fill it with a suggested answer |
| A hypothetical ("I would...") | "Has that happened yet? Tell me about that time" |

Keep a list of their exact words for the struggle. Those phrases are often better copy than anything the team writes.

## Force table and coding rules

One row per interview:

| Id | Switched from | First-thought trigger | Push | Pull | Anxiety | Habit | Alternatives, nearly chose | Deciding event |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I-01 | Spreadsheet | Month-end close missed by two days | Manual reconciliation | One-click export to finance | Losing historic data | "The sheet worked for years" | Hiring a contractor; nearly chose doing nothing | New finance lead asked for audit trail |

Coding rules:

1. Code each interview alone first, then cluster. Clustering while coding pulls every later interview toward the first theme you named.
2. A theme name uses the customer's words where possible.
3. Count interviews per theme, not mentions. One talkative person mentioning a theme five times is one interview.
4. Report "5 of 8"; do not convert to a percentage.
5. Have the second interviewer code two interviews independently. Where the two disagree on a force, discuss the quote rather than averaging; the disagreement usually shows a theme name that is too broad.
6. Keep a column for the alternative they nearly chose. A recurring near-choice is a competitor or a workaround you are underestimating.
