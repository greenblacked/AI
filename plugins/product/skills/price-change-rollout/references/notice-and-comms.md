# Notice, communication and close-out

Contents: the timeline, the customer notice, the internal FAQ, the exceptions log, and the close-out checklist.

## Timeline

Work backwards from each cohort's effective date. For an annual B2B cohort migrating at renewal with 90 days' notice:

| When | What | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Effective date minus about 120 days | Cohort model and policy signed off; contract terms checked by legal | Product and finance |
| Minus about 105 days | Support macros, sales and success FAQ, exceptions policy published internally; billing change built and tested on test accounts | Support lead, billing owner |
| At least 10 days before the written notice (about minus 100 days) | Account owners call top accounts, so no top account learns of it from the bulk notice | Account owners |
| Minus 90 days or the contract's notice period, whichever is longer | Written notice to every account in the cohort | Lifecycle or billing team |
| Minus 30 days | Reminder notice and in-product banner for admins and billing contacts | Product |
| Effective date | Billing change applies at renewal | Billing owner |
| First billing cycle | Sample of real invoices checked by hand; failed payments and disputes watched | Finance |
| Review date | Tripwire review per cohort | Named decider |

Monthly self-serve cohorts compress this, but keep the order: internal readiness, then notice, then the change, then the check.

## Customer notice template

Plain, specific to the cohort, with the date that applies to that customer rather than a general launch date.

> Subject: Your [product] price from [effective date]
>
> From [effective date], the price of your [plan] subscription will change from [current price] to [new price] per [period]. For your account this takes effect at your renewal on [renewal date]; nothing changes before then.
>
> Why: [one or two sentences, true and specific: what has been added or what costs have changed].
>
> Your options:
>
> - Renew for [term] at your current price if you commit before [date].
> - Move to [other plan] at [price], which includes [what it includes].
> - Talk to [named person or team] at [contact].
>
> [Sign-off from a named person.]

Do not bury the price in a feature announcement. A customer who has to work out that their price went up will assume it was hidden on purpose.

## Internal FAQ

Questions support and account teams will be asked, with agreed answers:

- Why is the price changing?
- When does it apply to me, exactly?
- Can I keep my current price? (The lock-in offer, its deadline and its term.)
- Is there a cheaper plan? (What it includes and what it does not.)
- I was promised this price would never change. (Escalate to the account owner with the contract; check whether a clause actually says so.)
- I want to cancel. (The cancellation path, and whether a retention offer exists and who may make it.)

## Exceptions log

Every exception granted is recorded: account, cohort, what was granted, until when, who approved it, and why. Exceptions follow the same approval ladder as other discounts, so a price change does not turn into a new source of unapproved discounting. Review the log at the tripwire review; a large number of exceptions in one cohort is a reaction signal in its own right.

## Close-out checklist

- Every cohort has reached the new price or passed its sunset date.
- Old prices removed from billing plans, the public price list, quote templates and the sales configuration tool.
- Grandfathered accounts with exceptions have an end date recorded, or a recorded decision to keep them.
- Support macros and help-centre pages updated to the new prices.
- Tripwire review recorded, with the per-cohort result and any rollback taken.
