## Selection scorecard

Score each candidate 0, 1 or 2 per row. Agree the rows before looking at names, so a famous logo cannot reshape the criteria.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Segment fit (size, industry, tools) | Outside | Edge | Squarely inside |
| Has the problem now | No recent example | Example older than a quarter | Example from the last month |
| Named person with time | None | Named, time unclear | Named and confirmed 30 minutes weekly |
| Sponsor agrees to the give-get | No | Verbally | In writing |
| Real data or environment | Demo only | Partial or delayed | Real, from the first session |
| Can buy at the end | No budget path | Budget possible | Budget exists |

A 0 on segment fit or on named person with time rules a candidate out whatever the total: otherwise a candidate outside the segment who scores 2 everywhere else (10) outranks one squarely inside it on 7, and the programme stops describing one segment. Break a tie at the cut-off on segment fit, then on how recent the problem is. Take the highest remaining scorers until you have five to eight, then check the set: no more than one or two from any single sub-type, and no partner whose loss would end the programme. Keep the next scorers on a waiting list.

## Give-get agreement outline

Have your legal team turn this into the agreement; it lists what must be covered, not the wording.

1. Term, start and end dates.
2. Partner commitments: named participant, weekly 30-minute session, feedback turnaround in days, real data or environment, reference or case study at the end if the agreed outcome is met.
3. Your commitments: early access, a named owner, the monthly decision log, pricing at conversion (amount or discount and for how long).
4. What is not promised: specific features, dates, exclusivity, custom work.
5. Confidentiality in both directions, including pre-release features.
6. Data: what you access, where it is stored, who can see it, and return or deletion at the end.
7. Pre-release terms: no service levels, support during business hours only, or whatever you can actually offer.
8. Attendance: a warning after the first missed session that is not rescheduled; the partnership ends at the second.
9. Ending: either side may end with notice; what happens to data and access when it ends.

## Session agenda

Thirty minutes, weekly, same slot.

| Minutes | Item |
| --- | --- |
| 0-5 | What changed since last session, from the decision log |
| 5-20 | The current build or prototype, used on their data, with them driving |
| 20-27 | Their last real occasion of the job since the previous session: what they did, what broke |
| 27-30 | Anything they need from you; confirm next session |

Notes template:

```markdown
# [Partner id] -- session [n] -- [date]
Attendees:
Changed since last time:
Observed on their data:
Last real occasion (what happened, verbatim where possible):
Requests raised (job and context, sent to the request queue):
Follow-ups (owner, date):
```

## Decision log template

| Date | Decision or change | Not changed, and why | Partners whose input drove it | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-11-12 | Import runs nightly instead of on demand | Real-time sync deferred | P-02, P-04, P-05 | Session notes 6 and 7 |

Share the log monthly with every partner, filtered to remove other partners' confidential details.

## Exit checklist

### Convert

- Contract on the pricing agreed at the start.
- Move from pre-release to a supported plan; confirm data carries over.
- Thank the participant and sponsor; show their entries in the decision log.

#### Keep as a reference

- Agree the form: case study, reference calls (how many per quarter), quote.
- Get written approval of any published wording.
- Name an owner who protects their time from too many calls.

##### Release

- Written thank-you listing what changed because of them.
- Return or delete their data as the agreement says, and confirm in writing.
- Turn off pre-release access on a stated date.
- Ask whether they would like to hear when the product reaches general availability.
