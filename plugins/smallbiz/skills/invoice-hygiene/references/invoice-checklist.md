## Pre-send checklist

| Line | Check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Number | Next in the series, no gap, no reuse | Auditors and clients test completeness |
| Date of issue | Today, matching the trigger event | Starts the payment clock |
| Supplier | Legal name, address, tax registration number if registered | Required content in many jurisdictions |
| Client | Legal name and billing address from the contract | Matches the vendor record |
| Reference | PO, cost centre or contract number | Often the only key the approver uses |
| Description | Milestone or period, quantity, unit price | Lets the approver tick it against the order |
| Tax | Rate, amount and wording as the tax authority requires | A defective tax line can make the invoice unusable for the client's own tax claim |
| Total and currency | Net, tax, gross in one currency | Avoids a short payment |
| Due date | A calendar date plus the stated terms | Removes the "net 30 from when" argument |
| Payment details | Account name equal to payee name, identifiers for the client's country | Name mismatches hold payments |
| Late-payment rule | Agreed fee or statutory interest with source | Only if one exists |
| File | PDF, number in file name | Keeps the trail searchable |

## Supplier-details form

Send this to the client before the first invoice and keep their answer.

1. Exact legal name and billing address to invoice.
2. Accounts-payable email or portal link, and whether PDF is accepted.
3. Do you need a PO or reference on each invoice? Who issues it?
4. Tax registration number you hold for us to quote, if applicable.
5. Standard payment terms and the day of the week or month you run payments.
6. Who approves invoices, and how long does approval usually take?
7. Do you require a structured e-invoice format?

## Credit note procedure

1. Issue a credit note for the full value of the wrong invoice, quoting its number.
2. Issue a new invoice with the next number and the corrected content.
3. Send both together with one line saying which replaces which and the new due date.
4. File the three documents together. If the client has already paid part, the credit note
   and the new invoice net off in the books; tell the client the balance.

## Diagnosing a rejected or missing invoice

- Never arrived: check the address, the spam folder on their side, and ask for a
  confirmed receipt. Resend to accounts payable.
- Rejected: ask for the reason in writing. Common causes are a missing PO, a legal name
  mismatch, a missing tax number and a wrong submission channel.
- Approved but unpaid: ask for the payment run date. After the due date, use
  `late-payment-chase`.
