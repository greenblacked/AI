## Account reconciliation worksheet

| Account | Statement closing balance | Books closing balance | Difference | Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Also reconcile the opening balance to last year's closing figure. A difference that
carries from the previous year means an earlier error; tell the accountant instead of
adjusting this year to hide it. Typical causes: transactions dated either side of year
end, transfers between your own accounts booked on one side only, processor payouts
booked gross, cash withdrawals not categorised.

## Income walk

Start: total invoiced for the year (net of any sales tax or VAT if you are registered).

| Step | Amount | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invoiced in the year | | Invoice list |
| Add: income not invoiced (interest, grants, marketplace sales) | | Statements |
| Less: receivables at year end | | Receivable list |
| Add: receivables from last year collected | | Last year's list |
| Add: deposits received for next year | | Contracts |
| Expected deposits | | |
| Actual deposits | | Bank and processor |
| Difference | | |

Common explained differences: processor fees and chargebacks, refunds and credit notes,
sales tax collected and held for the authority, foreign-currency differences, owner money
paid in, loan proceeds. Anything left over goes on the gap list.

## Receipt test

Find the substantiation threshold on your tax authority's website and note the source and
date. Filter expenses above it and add unusual categories (travel, meals, gifts, large
equipment, anything paid in cash).

| Date | Payee | Amount | Document | Status (supported / replaceable / unsupported) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

A document supports an expense when it shows supplier, date, amount and what was bought.
A card statement line alone usually does not.
