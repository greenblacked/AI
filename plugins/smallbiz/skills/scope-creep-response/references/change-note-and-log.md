# Change note and change log

## The change note

Keep it to three lines plus the choice. The client reads the top and decides; detail goes below the line only if asked.

```text
Subject: Change request: [short name], and your options

Thanks for this. It is not part of the agreed scope ([document, section]), so here is
what it would involve.

What is new: [one sentence]
Cost and hours: about [n] hours, [amount] fixed, added to the [milestone] invoice
Date impact: [delivery stays on X | delivery moves from X to Y]

Your options:
1. Add it at the above cost and date.
2. Swap it for [item of similar size] so price and date stay as they are.
3. Park it for a second phase, which I will price when this one is delivered.

I will start as soon as you reply with the option you want.
```

## Wording for the three options

- **Add.** State the new total and the new date together, so the date cost is not discovered later.
- **Swap.** Propose the swap yourself and name a candidate with a similar hour count. A client choosing between two things they want learns the project has a size.
- **Park.** Record it in the log with the status "phase two" and send the list at handover. It is a promise to price, not to deliver.

## When the client pushes back

- "It is only a small thing." If it is under your favour threshold, grant it as a logged courtesy. If it is over, quote the hours and offer the swap.
- "I assumed it was included." Quote the scope sentence, then ask whether they want the option 1 price or to look at the swap. Avoid arguing about intent.
- "Just do it and we will sort out the cost later." Reply that you will start as soon as the cost is agreed in writing, and give the time that will take. Work done before agreement is the work you are least likely to be paid for.
- "Every change you propose costs extra." Show the log: how many requests were absorbed, and their value.

## The change log

One row per request, including in-scope and courtesy rows.

| Column | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Date | When the request arrived |
| Request | One line, client's words |
| Class | In scope, clarification, change, courtesy |
| Decision | Added, swapped, parked, declined by client, granted |
| Hours | Estimated hours, and actual when finished |
| Price | Amount, or the full value for a courtesy |
| Date impact | Days moved, or none |
| Billed | Billed with invoice N, courtesy, or parked |
| Approval | Link or filename of the written yes |

Beneath the table keep three totals: approved billed changes, courtesies at full price, and parked items.

## Reconciling the final invoice

1. Take the original fixed price.
2. Add every billed change from the log, each with its written approval.
3. Show courtesies as zero with their value, so the client sees the total they were given.
4. Confirm that the sum of invoices issued equals the sum above.
5. Send the log with the final invoice. A client who has seen each note will not be surprised by the total.
