## Worked rate calculation

Figures below are invented to show the arithmetic only.

```text
Last year's profit before tax:                 50,000
Income tax for the year:                        6,500
Social contributions for the year:              4,200
Total on profit:                               10,700

Effective rate: 10,700 / 50,000 = 21.4%
Margin: +4 points
Sweep rate: 25.4%, rounded up to 26%
```

The rate is tax over profit, so sweeping it on receipts over-saves by the cost share (with costs at 40% of receipts, about 15.6% of receipts is needed); the quarterly check releases the excess. Sweeping 26% of a payment of 1,000 excluding sales tax moves 260. If the payment was 1,200 including 200 of sales tax, 200 goes to the tax-collected bucket and 260 to the income-tax account, leaving 740 as spendable before business costs.

Points to check:

- Use the same year for every figure. A tax charged on one year's profit paid in the next must be matched back to the profit that caused it.
- If last year included an unusual item, adjust profit and tax for it before dividing; otherwise the rate is distorted.
- If this year's profit is expected to be much higher, compute the marginal effect rather than reusing the average: the extra profit is taxed at the top bracket you reach.

## Provisional rate with no history

Choose a rate above what you expect, check it at the first quarter, and write the review date in the plan. At the first quarter:

1. Add up the profit so far (income received minus costs paid).
2. Estimate the year's profit by scaling, then lowering for known quiet months.
3. Work out the tax due on that estimate from the authority's published rates, or ask an adviser.
4. Divide by the profit to get the effective rate, add margin and replace the provisional rate.

## Calendar template

| Due date | What is due | Source URL | Date checked | Prep reminder (3 weeks before) | Internal deadline (5 working days before) | Amount needed | Balance on prep date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| | | | | | | | |

Rules for filling it in:

- One row per payment, including returns for VAT or sales tax even when the amount is a refund.
- The URL must be the authority's own page. Recheck the page each January and after any announced change.
- Weekends and public holidays shorten the five working days; count backwards on a calendar.
- Put the reminders in a calendar app with an alert, not just a spreadsheet.

If the change exceeds 15% in either direction, recompute the rate. If it is under 15%, leave the rate and note that you checked. Write one line explaining any change, so next year you can see why.
