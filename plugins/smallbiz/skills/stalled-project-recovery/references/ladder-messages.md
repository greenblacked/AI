# Ladder messages

Each message names the exact item, asks for one thing and gives a date. Edit to your voice and the client's.

## Log layout

Waiting on: [item] | Asked on: [date] | Due: [date] | Day 0: [date]

date | day | channel | person | message sent | reply

## Day 3: nudge

> Hi [name], I am ready to carry on with [stage] as soon as I have [exact item]. Can you reply with [yes/no or option] or upload it here: [link]?

## Day 7: default

> Hi [name], following up on [item]. To keep the schedule, I will proceed with [option A] on [date] unless I hear otherwise. If you would prefer [option B], reply by then and I will switch; changes after that date may affect the timeline and cost.

## Day 14: other channel

Call or text the same person, or another named contact. Open with the single question, not a complaint. Afterwards email:

> Thanks for talking today (or: I tried to reach you today). To confirm: I need [item] by [date] to continue. If the project is on hold on your side, tell me and I will plan around it.

## Day 21: formal pause

> Hi [name], I asked for [item] on [date] and followed up on [dates]. As I have not had a reply, I am pausing work on the project and releasing the reserved time. Under [clause / our agreement] restarting will need [fee, if the agreement provides] and a new start date, to be confirmed on availability. I have attached an invoice for [milestone already delivered]. When you are ready, reply and I will send available dates.

## Day 45: close-out

> Hi [name], as the project has been on hold since [date], I am closing it out. Attached are an invoice for work completed to date and the files delivered so far: [list]. Anything not yet done is listed below. If you would like to resume, we can agree new terms and dates.

## Checks before sending any rung

- Is the item really the client's to supply, or did you promise something first?
- Does the agreement set a different number of days?
- Is anything you are about to hand over unpaid and outside what the contract allows?
