## A worked calculation

Currency units are arbitrary; swap in your own.

| Line | Amount per year |
| --- | --- |
| Target take-home | 45,000 |
| Business costs: software 1,200, insurance 600, accountant 900, equipment 600, training 500, workspace 1,800 | 5,600 |
| Pension saving the employer used to match | 3,000 |
| Health and income-protection cover | 1,400 |
| Costs subtotal (paid from revenue, before tax) | 10,000 |

Tax is a share of profit. If the profit needed to deliver 45,000 after tax at an
assumed 25% effective rate is 45,000 / (1 - 0.25) = 60,000, then:

Required revenue = 60,000 + 10,000 = 70,000

Hours: 52 weeks - 4 weeks leave - 2 weeks public holidays - 1.5 weeks sick = 44.5 working
weeks. At 40 hours a week and a tracked 55% billable share:

44.5 x 40 x 0.55 = 979 billable hours.

- Floor = 70,000 / 979 = about 71.5 per hour
- Target, with a 20% premium (two clients pay at 45+ days, contracts are short) = about 86
- Walk-away, set at floor plus 10% = about 79

Day rate for a billed day defined as eight hours of client work = 86 x 8 = 688. The hourly
rate already carries the unbillable share, so a shorter contracted day lowers the day rate
in proportion (six hours would give 516, which is correct only if the agreement says six).

The tax rate in this example is a placeholder. Take the real figure from the accountant;
if profit crosses a tax band the effective rate rises, and the required revenue with it.

## The four-week tracking log

One row per day, hours in each column:

| Date | Billable | Sales and proposals | Admin and invoicing | Learning | Gaps and other |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

At the end of four weeks, billable share = total billable hours / total hours worked,
across every column. Note the weeks that were unusually busy or quiet. If the four weeks
were atypical, track four more before trusting the number.

## Minimum project size

Minimum project = overhead hours x floor / acceptable overhead share.

Example: eight hours to win, onboard and invoice a job, floor 71.5, accept losing 20% of
the job to overhead: 8 x 71.5 / 0.20 = 2,860. Jobs below about 2,900 are better
handled by a minimum charge, a bundled package or a decline.

## Variables that depend on the country

Name each as unknown rather than guessing: the effective tax and social-contribution rate
on profit, whether VAT or sales tax is charged on top of the rate (it is not income),
pension contribution limits, statutory sick pay for the self-employed (often none), and
whether professional indemnity is mandatory in your field. These change the required
revenue, so confirm them with an accountant before quoting the final number.
