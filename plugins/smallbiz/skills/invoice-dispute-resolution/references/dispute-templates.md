# Dispute templates

Keep every message factual, dated and short. No adjectives, no threats beyond the stated next step.

## Step 1: clarifying message

> Hi [name], thanks for telling me. So that I can address it properly, could you tell me in writing: (1) which part of invoice [number] you are questioning, (2) what outcome you are looking for, and (3) which requirement in our agreement ([scope document, date]) you feel was not met? In the meantime I would be grateful if [undisputed amount] could be paid by the due date of [date].

## Offer 1: defined correction

> Having reviewed [item] against the agreed scope, I can [specific fix]. I will deliver it by [date, within five working days]. Please confirm within [three working days] of receiving it whether it resolves the point; if it does, I would ask for payment of the invoice within seven days of your confirmation.

## Offer 2: itemised partial settlement

> To resolve this, I propose the following against invoice [number]: line [x] reduced by [amount] because [reason]; lines [y, z] unchanged. I will issue credit note [number] against invoice [number], leaving that invoice unchanged: net [amount], tax [amount] at the original rate of [rate], gross [amount]. The balance due is [original gross minus credit note gross], payable by [date, within seven days of your acceptance]. On receipt, the invoice is settled in full and neither of us will pursue the matter further. If you agree, please reply to confirm.

Worked example, at an example tax rate of 20% (use the rate on the original invoice): invoice 1,200 net plus 240 tax is 1,440 gross. An agreed reduction of 300 net gives a credit note of 300 net, 60 tax (300 x 20%) and 360 gross. The balance due is 1,440 - 360 = 1,080. If you are registered for VAT or an equivalent sales tax, the 60 reduces the output tax you owe; check your tax authority for when and how to adjust the return. The original invoice is not edited.

## Step 3: classification

- Defect: the delivered work does not meet the written scope or stated standard. Fix it or reduce.
- Change of mind: the work meets the scope; the client now wants something else. Offer a change order, not a discount.
- Out of scope expectation: the requirement was never written. Show the scope; offer the work as a quoted extra if it is worth doing.
