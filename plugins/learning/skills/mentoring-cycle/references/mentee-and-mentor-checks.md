# Mentee and mentor checks

Read at step 7, or when sessions feel stale.

## The question ladder with examples

| Rung | Question | Example use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | What have you tried so far? | The mentee asks how to handle a stakeholder who ignores them |
| 2 | What do you expect to happen, and why? | The mentee plans to escalate; ask what they expect the outcome to be |
| 3 | What would you do if I were not here? | Surfaces the answer they already hold |
| 4 | What would change your mind? | Tests how firm the plan is |

If a rung gets silence, wait. Rephrase once, not twice. Advice follows rung 3 and is framed as one experience.

## For the mentee

- Send the agenda even when the week was quiet; a short one is fine.
- Bring a decision, not a topic. "Should I take the migration lead role?" gives the mentor something to work with.
- Do the actions. A mentor who sees them dropped twice will reasonably stop investing.
- Ask for what you want: a challenge, a sounding board, an introduction.
- Use the ending sentence early if it is not working.

## Repair steps

| Symptom | Likely cause | Change to try |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sessions are chats | Goals are topics, or no agenda | Rewrite goals as observable abilities; require the agenda |
| The mentor talks most of the time | Advice before questions | Hold to the ladder; track speaking time for one session |
| Actions never done | Too many, or too vague | Cut to one; add a date and a first step |
| Sessions keep being cancelled | Cadence is wrong or interest has gone | Raise it at once; offer the ending sentence |
| Mentee wants the mentor to speak for them | Boundary blurred | Offer to help prepare, not to represent |
| Mentee raises a conduct or safety issue | Outside the contract | Apply the stated exception; say so openly |
| Same topic every session | The real goal is not named | Ask what the mentee would change if the topic were solved |

## When the mentor is also senior to the mentee's manager

Agree in the contract that nothing is passed up. If the mentor hears something about the mentee's team that they cannot unhear, they say so and do not act on it without the mentee's agreement unless an exception applies.
