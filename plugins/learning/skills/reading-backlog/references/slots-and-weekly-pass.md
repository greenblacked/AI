# Slots and the weekly pass

Read at steps 7 to 9.

## Slot sizes by format

| Format | Slot | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Article or post | 25 minutes | Read it whole in the slot or archive it |
| Research paper | 45 to 60 minutes | Abstract, conclusion and the results table first; stop at the abandon rule |
| Book chapter | 30 minutes, recurring | Three to five a week beats a weekend block |
| Report or long document | 60 minutes | Set the 30-page abandon line before starting |
| Video or talk | Length plus ten minutes | Count it as an article if it fits a slot |

Book slots in the calendar as real appointments, against a named open item. Choose times that already exist: a commute, the first half hour after lunch, the end of the working day. A slot that needs a new routine is a habit project, which is a different skill.

## The weekly pass (ten minutes)

Run it at the same time each week, with a timer.

1. Open the inbox of new saves. For each, ask: does it serve a task this quarter? If no, archive.
2. For each that does, can it enter the queue? The queue holds ten items at most; if it is full, the new item waits or replaces one that has been finished, dropped or archived. Starting it is a separate question: only three items may be open.
3. For each open item, ask: is it booked in a slot this week? If not, book it or drop it.
4. Count the queue. If it is over ten, archive from the bottom.
5. Write the week's number: items finished or dropped.

## The monthly measure

```markdown
| Month | Finished | Dropped | Archived | Queue at month end |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| October | 4 | 2 | 31 | 7 |
```

The finished and dropped columns are the signal. A queue that stays at 7 while finished rises is working. If finished is flat for two months, change one thing: shorten the queue, lengthen the slots or move them to a better time. Do not add a new tool.

## When a deadline arrives

A task with a date can promote any archived item. Pull it out by search, name the task, and let it replace an open item. The rules still apply: three at a time.
